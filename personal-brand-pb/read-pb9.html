01The lines, and when each opens to a small account

Six kinds of income are open to an account like yours. They do not open at the same time, and the order is set by who decides you are eligible:

  • Lines you open yourself, on day one. An own product (digital or physical), services and teaching, and a paid membership or newsletter on a third-party tool need no audience threshold from either platform; they need a payment tool, the legal lines in PB8's register, and someone willing to pay.
  • Lines a programme or a brand opens. Affiliate programmes accept or refuse you on their own terms; brand deals open when a brand asks or accepts a pitch. Outside the platforms' own tools, neither platform sets a follower count for them; YouTube's own affiliate and partnership tools have thresholds, and Instagram publishes none (§2).
  • Lines a platform opens at a threshold. YouTube's fan funding at 500 subscribers and 3,000 watch hours, its ad revenue share at 1,000 subscribers and, for new applicants from 1 February 2027, 8,000 watch hours (§2). Instagram publishes no threshold for its earning tools (§2).

The sections below take the lines in another order, starting with platform revenue share; the figure gives the order in which they open.

02Platform revenue share

YouTube

The thresholds, as YouTube states them (checked 13 November 2026). From 1 February 2027, new creators applying to the YouTube Partner Programme need "8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days", with 1,000 subscribers; channels already in the programme keep their status ("If you are already in YPP, your status is not impacted by this update"), but to keep earning from the associated features they must "review and accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027"; without that they "stop earning from the associated monetization features beginning February 1, 2027", a decision YouTube says "does not impact your channel’s status in the YouTube Partner Program" (YouTube Help, Changes to the YouTube Partner Program, answer 12843009). Platform-official. Until that date the programme's current page gives the old route, 1,000 subscribers "with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months" (YouTube Help, Overview of the expanded YouTube Partner Program, answer 13429240).

A lower tier opens first. Channels with "500 subscribers and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in the last year or 3M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days" can use fan funding, YouTube Creator Partnerships and YouTube Shopping, and these thresholds do not change in 2027 (answer 12843009). The expanded-programme page adds "3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days" and names what opens at 500: channel memberships, Super Chat and Super Stickers, Super Thanks, Jewels and gifts, and Shopping; Portugal is among the listed countries (answer 13429240). Platform-official.

Shorts are a separate line. From 1 February 2027 a channel earns from the Shorts Creator Pool only if it maintains "10M qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days"; missing it does not remove a channel from the programme or affect its other earnings (answer 12843009). Mara posts no Shorts in year one (PB6, D1), so this line is zero for her and is not modelled.

What the platform pays when you are in. YouTube's announcement of these changes states the creator revenue share as "55% for long-form videos and 45% for Shorts" (YouTube Official Blog, The YouTube Team, 10 Aug 2026). For channel memberships, "Creators get 70% of membership revenue after applicable taxes and fees are deducted", and "All transaction costs (including credit card fees) are currently covered by YouTube" (YouTube Help, Analyze and manage your YouTube channel memberships program, answer 7491256). Platform-official.

What the thresholds mean in views. 8,000 hours is 480,000 minutes. vidIQ's guidance band for a 5–15 minute video is "aim for 40 to 55% viewed", a target it publishes without a sample (vidIQ, YouTube Average View Duration, updated 21 Jul 2026). On a 10-minute video that is 4 to 5.5 minutes a view, so the 8,000-hour line needs about 87,273 to 120,000 long-form views in 365 days, or 3,357 to 4,615 a video at 26 videos a year; the 3,000-hour line needs about 32,727 to 45,000 (derived). vidIQ's cohort of 137,355 new long-form channels had a median of about 272 views in 30 days on the tenth upload, and it says "Channels that gave up before publishing ten same-format videos in their first 90 days are excluded entirely" (vidIQ, YouTube First 10 Videos Study, 28 Jul 2026, updated 30 Jul 2026). Vendor data; survivor sample. The 8,000-hour line is about 12 to 17 times that median per video (derived). PB1 §4 reached the same figures, rounded, on 12 November 2026.

What the ad line would pay once open. AIR Media-Tech read the YouTube Analytics of 300 channels it works with, May 2025 to May 2026, and reports a median RPM (revenue per 1,000 views) of $2.39 for Crafting & Handmade and $2.98 for Lifestyle; it lists no home or DIY category, and the channels are its clients, not a random sample (AIR Media-Tech, Which YouTube niche makes the most money in 2026, last updated 1 Jul 2026). Vendor data. At $2.39, the 87,273 views that just clear the 8,000-hour line would pay about $208.58 a year (derived). The page's own date is inconsistent: dated 1 July 2026, it refers to September 2026.

Shopping affiliate. YouTube's own affiliate programme needs a channel in the Partner Programme, at its subscriber threshold, based in one of 18 listed countries (Argentina, Brazil, Germany, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, the Netherlands, the Philippines, Poland, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, the United Kingdom, the United States and Vietnam); "Every participating brand and retailer will set their commission rates and attribution window for each product" (YouTube Help, YouTube Shopping affiliate program overview & eligibility, answer 13376398). Platform-official. Portugal is not on the list, so this line is closed to Mara however large she grows, until the list changes.

Instagram

Instagram's page for creators names six earning tools (Subscriptions, Gifts, Partnership Ads, Creator Marketplace, Badges and Bonuses) and states no follower, age, country or account-type requirement for any of them (Instagram, Monetize with Instagram, checked 13 November 2026). Platform-official, by absence. PB1 found the same on 12 November 2026. No Instagram Help Centre page on Subscriptions or Gifts was returned by any search for this unit, so the rules were not read at Instagram's own source (as PB7 and PB8 also found). A vendor guide updated 23 July 2026 says "Meta's own documentation is not consistent on this and access appears to be invite-based and genuinely uneven", and that "Meta takes 0% of subscription revenue", with app stores taking "roughly 30% for a subscriber's first year"; it cites no Meta page for any of it (Inro, Instagram Subscriptions, updated 23 Jul 2026). Vendor claim, unsourced; not used in the model.

So the place an Instagram account reads its own eligibility is the professional dashboard in the app, at each checkpoint (PB1, D3 read 6). Facebook's rules are a different platform's: its subscriptions page requires, for a Facebook Page, "10,000 followers or 250+ Return Viewers" and "50,000 Post Engagements" or "180,000 Watch Minutes" in the last 60 days (Meta for Creators, Subscriptions, checked 13 November 2026); it says nothing about Instagram, and nothing here applies it to Instagram.

For Mara. She needs 3,357 to 4,615 views a video for the ad line and about 1,259 to 1,731 for fan funding (derived, at 26 videos), against a median tenth upload of 272 in a cohort that posted faster than she does and excludes quitters (PB1, D1 row Y1). These are PB1's rows Y10 and Y11 (PB1, D1), with the terms-acceptance condition above applying only to channels already in the programme. Both YouTube lines are zero in every scenario of her year-one model. The Instagram line is zero because no rule and no rate is published; if the dashboard shows a tool as available at a checkpoint, it is recorded and priced then.

03Affiliate links

An affiliate link pays a commission on purchases made after a click. The rates are published; the conversion is not.

Rates. Amazon's US programme pays fixed rates by category: 3.00% for "Toys, Furniture, Home, Home Improvement, Lawn & Garden" and, in the same band, "Tools"; 4.50% for "Physical Books, Kitchen, Automotive"; 4.00% for "All Other Categories" (Amazon Associates, Standard Commission Income Statement, checked 13 November 2026; the page shows no effective date). Platform-official, for the US store; each Amazon store and each retailer publishes its own schedule. YouTube's Shopping affiliate rates are set per brand (§2).

Conversion. No source found that meets this course's standard gives the share of a small creator's viewers who click an affiliate link, or the share of clicks that buy, on either platform. The nearest is a trade report of a vendor's analysis of more than 10,000 mostly US YouTube creator integrations (October 2023 to December 2025): by the eighth integration with the same creator, "affiliate link CTR reaches 1.8x the rate of the first integration" (EMARKETER, reporting Agentio); it gives no base rate and no purchase rate. Vendor data, secondary report; not used in the model.

What it would pay at Mara's size. As an illustration only (Mara, fictional; no source gives any of the inputs except the rate): one qualifying order per video, at an order of USD 30, on 13 videos, at Amazon US's 3.00% for tools, is USD 11.70 in a year (derived: 13 × 30 × 0.03). The cost is not in money. Under the UK regulator's guidance, commissions count as payment ("Any form of incentive or reward – including money, commissions, discounts") and "It is not enough just to tag a brand in your post, use discount codes or affiliate links" (CMA, Social media endorsements: guidance for content creators, updated 3 Sep 2025); the FTC says a disclosure "should be in the video and not just in the description" (FTC, Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers, Nov 2019, modified 3 Dec 2024). Regulator guidance. A video with affiliate links therefore carries an ad label in the video, and a channel that puts them in every description labels every video an ad (PB8 §6.1 sets out the EU, UK and US rules in full; this unit's reading of the two guides together).

04Brand deals

What brands pay, as measured. Collabstr, a marketplace, reports from "more than 21,000 collaborations" on its platform in 2025 an average paid of $255 for YouTube and $193 for Instagram, against asking prices of $311 and $214 (Collabstr, 2026 Influencer Marketing Report; no publication date on the page). Vendor data; its own users; no breakdown by follower count; the page says YouTube payouts are "down 63% year over year from $418", which its own figures do not give (418 to 255 is a fall of 39%, derived). A companion article gives "Micro-influencers (10K–100K followers) average $198 per collaboration" and no figure for smaller accounts (Collabstr, What influencers really cost in 2026). Vendor data.

Whether brands want accounts your size. Influencer Marketing Hub's 2026 survey of "600+" marketing professionals reports that 51.43% of respondents indicate expansion intent for nano creators (42.86% planning an increase, 8.57% planning to start with the tier); it does not define nano or give a share of brands that work with them (Influencer Marketing Hub, Influencer Marketing Benchmark Report 2026, 4 May 2026). Vendor survey; intentions, not deals. No source found gives the rate at which accounts under 1,000 followers or subscribers receive paid offers, or what they are paid.

Platform tools. YouTube's Creator Partnerships opens with the 500-subscriber tier (§2). YouTube's August 2026 announcement mentions "incentives for brand deals" for channels below its Shorts threshold with "We'll share more details soon" (YouTube Official Blog, 10 Aug 2026); this unit did not find the details. Instagram's Creator Marketplace is listed with no terms (§2).

What a deal costs you. A deal needs the label in the post and the platform's tool (PB8, D1 clause 7 and register lines A1–A3), and PB8 §7 sends the first brand contract to a lawyer, whose fee no unit of this course has priced. A gifted product may count as income (PB8 register line T2, open). The disclosure itself lowered brand attitudes and credibility in a meta-analysis of "61 papers that use 57 distinct data sets" (Eisend et al., Journal of Advertising 49(3), 2020); that study measures the effect of a label on one piece, not the effect of how many sponsored pieces an account carries, which §11 takes up. Peer-reviewed; abstract read.

05An own product, digital and physical

Digital. A guide, a template or a recorded lesson costs its making once and nothing per sale beyond the fee. Gumroad charges "10% + $0.50" on sales through your own links, 30% on sales found through its marketplace, and no monthly fee, and states "Gumroad is your merchant of record", handling VAT and similar taxes since 1 January 2025 (Gumroad, Pricing, checked 13 November 2026); the page does not say whether card processing is inside the 10%. beehiiv includes "Digital Products" on every plan, its Free plan too, with "beehiiv takes 0%" and "Stripe's standard processing fee of 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction" (beehiiv, Pricing, checked 13 November 2026); it does not say it is the merchant of record. Platform-official.

Conversion. No source found that meets the standard gives the share of a creator's audience or email list that buys a creator's own digital product, on either platform or by email. Any number of sales in a plan is the planner's own until the first sales replace it.

Physical. A physical product adds stock, packing and delivery. Etsy's seller page lists a "$0.20 Listing fee", a "6.5 % Transaction fee" on the sale price including the shipping you set, and a "3% + $0.25 payment processing fee", which varies by bank country (Etsy, Sell on Etsy, checked 13 November 2026). Platform-official. §8 shows why stock bought ahead of sales is the risk at small numbers.

The tax question a sale raises. Who charges VAT or sales tax on a sale to a buyer in another country, and who pays it? EU: since 1 July 2021 cross-border sales of electronic services to consumers fall under the One Stop Shop, with an EU-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 (European Commission, VAT One Stop Shop); the Commission's own page, to open first; the law behind it, the EU VAT Directive, was not opened for this unit. UK: "The place of supply of cross-border digital services is the consumer's location" (GOV.UK, VAT rules for supplies of digital services to consumers, updated 28 Mar 2022); regulator guidance. US: sales tax is set by each state, and the source to open is the revenue department of the state where the buyer is; the Supreme Court's judgment on remote sellers, South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), was not opened for this unit. A platform that acts as merchant of record takes the collection off you; your own income tax and registration stay yours (PB8 §6.5, register line T1). None of this is stated as your rule; ask a tax professional where you live before the first sale.

06A paid membership or newsletter

The share of a list that pays. beehiiv's report on paid newsletters, from "first-party data from thousands of publications on beehiiv, analyzed across the 2021-2026 period", gives "The median paid conversion rate across beehiiv is 0.62%", "For every 1,000 subscribers, about 6 are paying", medians of $10 a month and $100 a year, and monthly churn from 5.06% to 16.67% by industry (beehiiv, The State of Paid Newsletters 2026, 22 Jun 2026). Platform data; publications that chose to charge, on a platform that sells paid-newsletter tools; no breakdown by list size, and "conversion" is not formally defined. Two documented cases sit near it: Artificial Ignorance had 13,413 free and 121 paid subscribers on 1 January 2025, 0.90% (Artificial Ignorance, 11 Feb 2025), and beehiiv describes Generalist World as "a living community of 43,000 newsletter subscribers and 700+ paying members", at least 1.63% (beehiiv, The State of Newsletters 2026, 6 Jan 2026). Documented creator cases, self-reported; percentages derived. Patreon's 2025 State of Create figures, which other summaries quote, were not used: Patreon's own article gives none of them (Patreon, State of Create in 2025, 19 Feb 2025), and the full report was not returned by any search.

The step before it is unmeasured. No source gives the share of a creator's viewers or followers who join an email list (PB3 §5). A paid-tier forecast therefore starts from your own list count, never from your follower count.

07Services and teaching

Paid teaching (a live online class, a one-to-one video call on a reader's own piece) and services (restoring for clients) are open from day one. No source found that meets the standard gives the share of a small creator's audience that books paid time, or a rate for such time by audience size, on either platform. What is certain is the hours: every service hour is sold from the same week as the publishing system. PB1's stop rule protects the long-form cadence (PB1, D3), and a service line that takes hours from it puts the account itself at risk. For a part-time creator a service line therefore starts only when its hours come from a named cut in the plan or from hours added to the budget at a checkpoint (PB3, D2 gives the 10-hour plan).

08Why sales to a small audience are small-number events

When a few people each decide independently whether to buy, the count of sales in a period behaves like a Poisson count: the NIST handbook gives it as the model for "the number of events occurring within a given time interval", with mean λ and standard deviation √λ (NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, §1.3.6.6.19). Whether buyers act independently is an assumption; a single mention by a larger account would break it. Three consequences follow (all derived):

  1. The spread is large relative to the count. The relative spread is 1 ÷ √λ: ±50% around an expected 4 sales, ±35% around 8, ±10% around 100.
  2. Empty months are normal. At an expected 1.165 sales a month, 31.2% of months have none; at 0.291 a month, 74.7%. A month with no sales says almost nothing about the line.
  3. The 5th to 95th percentile of outcomes: 0 to 5 sales when 2 are expected, 4 to 13 when 8 are, 16 to 32 when 24 are, and 84 to 117 when 100 are.

For stock. Suppose you buy 8 units of a physical product because 8 sales is your central estimate. If 8 is right, the chance you sell fewer than 8 is 45.3%, and the chance you sell 4 or fewer, half the stock left, is 10.0% (derived). And 8 is not known: no source gives a conversion rate for a creator's own product (§5), so the expected value is itself a guess. Stock bought ahead is a bet on two unknowns at once. Made-to-order, print-on-demand or pre-order moves the bet to the buyer's side; a digital product has no stock at all.

For effort. A line is judged on a quarter's or a year's count, at a checkpoint, never on a month. Hours spent on a line whose expected count is in single figures buy a result that the next month may not show; the checkpoint review reads the line's total against the hours it took.

10When a line is worth starting

No study found tests when a creator should start any income line. The test below is this course's, built from the evidence above; each step names its basis.

  1. Is it open? The platform threshold is met (§2), the policy allows it (PB8, D1 clause 7), and the legal lines it depends on are done (PB8 register: T1 before any income, A3 before a deal, E3–E4 before commercial email).
  2. Does it need stock? If so, it waits for a count of your own sales, or it runs made-to-order (§8).
  3. Where do its hours come from? A named cut in the plan, never the long-form cadence (PB1, D3; PB3, D2).
  4. Does it cover its own cash costs at the low scenario? A line whose fixed cost (a $49 monthly plan, a lawyer's fee) exceeds its low-scenario net waits until the count that pays for it is your own.
  5. What does it do to the account? How many pieces it turns into labelled ads (§3, §11).
  6. Among the lines that pass, start the one with the highest net per hour in the base scenario. One line at a time, so that its count at the next checkpoint is readable.

11Keeping the account from becoming an advert

What the evidence on sponsorship load says. Li, Gao, Gu and Leung define an influencer's endorsement rate as the share of sponsored posts among all posts before a given sponsored post, and report "a consistent U-shaped relationship between endorsement rate and consumer engagement with sponsored posts" (Li, Gao, Gu and Leung, Journal of Marketing, 2026, doi:10.1177/00222429261422744). Peer-reviewed. In the authors' accepted manuscript, the Instagram field study (4,908 Australian influencers, 6,869 sponsored posts, focal posts from October 2022) puts the low point of engagement at an endorsement rate of 0.475 for comments and 0.393 for likes, and a Douyin study of 1,000 influencers puts it near 0.52; follower count was a control, not tested as a moderator (Li et al., accepted manuscript, PolyU repository). The authors attribute the U to two forces: a higher rate raises recognition of the brand and also suspicion of manipulative intent; the study also includes two experiments, with 168 and 208 participants. What it means at your size: an account that adds sponsored posts from a low base moves down the falling side of the U, where each added sponsored post goes with less engagement on sponsored posts, until somewhere between about two in five and one in two of all posts are sponsored. The study measures engagement with the sponsored posts, not growth, trust or retention of the account, and its accounts were mostly far larger than yours: the Instagram sample averaged 83,782.6 followers (smallest 630) and the Douyin sample's smallest had 939, with follower count a control and no result reported for accounts under 1,000. Correlational in the field studies.

What the disclosure studies add. Eisend et al. (§4) measured the cost of the label on a piece; they do not measure load, and this unit does not use them as evidence on it (PB8 §2).

What follows (this course's rules; basis above). Count your sponsored pieces as a share of all pieces, per season, per platform. Keep it on the low side of the U: one sponsored long-form video per season of six or seven is 14% to 17% (derived), far from the 39% to 48% where the Instagram study found engagement lowest. Affiliate links in every description would put every video in the labelled share (§3). An own product introduced as yours is also an ad under the UK guide's rule for brands you own (PB8 §6.1), so it is counted too.
