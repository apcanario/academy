Deep dive B3, How the best practitioners use the tool. Search first and open every primary source; date each fact. 'The Karpathy method' is a label coined by bloggers and newsletters, not by Karpathy; the unit says so and builds only from his own posts, talks, repositories and interviews, discarding any practice a secondary source attributes to him that his own words do not support (one blog cites an 'AISN 2026' talk of which no record can be found; treat such claims as unsourced). Cover, in this order: Karpathy's documented practice and what each piece actually says — the vibe-coding post (Feb 2025) and his later distinction between vibe coding and agentic engineering; the 'How I use LLMs' video (Feb 2025); the Software 3.0 talk at YC AI Startup School (Jun 2025: the autonomy slider, the generation–verification loop, keeping the agent on a leash, the context window as the lever); his endorsement of 'context engineering' over 'prompt engineering' (Jun 2025); the Dwarkesh Patel interview (Oct 2025) and the shift to agent-first coding he described in his own post in Jan 2026 (about 80% manual in November to about 80% agent-driven in December; reviewing as a separate skill from writing; the failure modes he lists); nanochat (Oct 2025) and autoresearch (Mar 2026: the loop, the one file the agent may edit, the five-minute budget, the keep-or-revert rule, the run of about 700 experiments and its reported gain); the LLM Knowledge Bases post (Apr 2026: raw sources → compiled Markdown wiki → lint → query, Markdown as the source of truth the human rarely edits, about 100 articles and 400,000 words navigated by index without a vector store); the Sequoia AI Ascent 2026 talk (verifiability decides what gets automated; delegate thinking, keep understanding); and his move to Anthropic (May 2026) as context for reading his later statements. Then the other methods that have a primary source and a named author, each stated from that source with its date and an evidence grade (measured, documented practice, or stated opinion): Anthropic's 'Building effective agents' (Dec 2024), 'Effective context engineering for AI agents' (Sep 2025) and the Claude Code best-practices post (Apr 2025); OpenAI's 'A practical guide to building agents' (Apr 2025) and its model prompting guides; Simon Willison's definition of vibe coding against AI-assisted programming (Mar 2025), his account of how he uses LLMs to write code (Mar 2025) and the lethal trifecta (Jun 2025); Ethan Mollick's four principles from Co-Intelligence (2024) and the centaur/cyborg finding of the BCG field experiment (Dell'Acqua et al. 2023, with its sample size and where performance fell); spec-driven development as practised (Harper Reed's codegen workflow, Feb 2025; GitHub Spec Kit, Sep 2025; AWS Kiro, Jul 2025); and the prompting literature with its limits (chain-of-thought, Wei et al. 2022; ReAct, Yao et al. 2022; self-consistency, Wang et al. 2022; where chain-of-thought does not help, Sprague et al. 2024). For every method: what it claims, what evidence it rests on, who it was written for, and whether it transfers from code to his work (course units, research, scripts, the vault, the memory system, the skills), decided case by case; where a method is for software only, say so and leave it there.
