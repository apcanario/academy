Greece: syllabus and prompts

This is the complete syllabus the reports were generated from. Each module has its drop-in prompt; copy it into a new chat to regenerate a report yourself, extend one, or compare a fresh answer against the one here. Where a report exists it is linked.

How to run it

  1. Do the modules in order. Each prompt assumes the ones before it. Skipping ahead is how the gaps got there in the first place.
  2. Tap copy prompt, paste the prompt into a new chat here, read the report, do the self-test at the end. If you score under 7/10, re-read before moving on.
  3. Tick the box when done. Progress is stored on this phone only.
  4. Every prompt asks for named primary sources and for the places where ancient accounts contradict each other. There is no single canonical version of any Greek myth; knowing the variants is what "knowing the myth" means.

Units 10, 11 and 12 are deep dives: optional, and best taken after the core material they extend (unit 4; module 6.7; modules 5.6, 6.5, 7.2 and 7.4) or after the capstone.

Honest scoping: no syllabus gets you "all" of it. The corpus is thousands of local variants, half of them surviving as one line in a scholiast. What this gets you is the complete skeleton, the main sources read in the right order, and enough structure that anything you meet later snaps into place.
