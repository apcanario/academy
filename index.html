Why history and myth together

the method of the track
μῦθοςmythos

In Homer, simply what someone says with authority: a speech, a command, a tale. By the fifth century it had come to mean the story you cannot check.

λόγοςlogos

A word, a count, an account. By the same century it meant the argued explanation, the one that gives its reasons and invites you to test them.

The Greeks drew that line themselves. Herodotus calls his work a historiē, an inquiry, and still reports what the Persians and Egyptians told him. Thucydides warns that his history leaves out the mythōdes, the storylike, and will seem less pleasant for it (1.22). Plato expels the poets from his ideal city and then ends the Republic with a myth of his own.

The tidy story of a clean progress "from mythos to logos" (the title of Wilhelm Nestle's book of 1940) is now itself treated as a modern myth: the Greeks never stopped telling myths; their philosophers used them, their historians argued with them, and their cities were governed by them. So each course reads a culture's myths and its history together, asks who told each story and what they gained by it, and keeps the evidence in view. The aim is not to debunk the myths but to understand them as well as the history. Later courses point back to earlier ones where they touch: Rome to Greece's units 7 and 10, Persia to units 6 and 11, Egypt to units 1 and 7.

How every course works

the constants
  1. One reader. Every course is written for the same person and read in order, on a phone, with the citations checked.
  2. Sources first. A unit names its sources and says where they disagree; a claim that cannot be sourced is marked unsourced or left out.
  3. One unit per chat, built in full. Each unit is written from a published brief or prompt, in its own chat, with its closing artefact: a self-test and carry-forward ideas for the history courses, a shipped deliverable for Brand + AI.
  4. Audited every four units. After every four units written in a hub, a stratified sample of claims is checked against its sources with a second model; a unit that fails gets a full audit. The model that wrote each unit is recorded.
  5. An open syllabus. Every prompt and brief is published with its course, so any unit can be regenerated, extended or checked.
