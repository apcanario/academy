Building with AI

For an experienced professional who reasons and writes well and uses a chat assistant with no method: how the tool works, which model for which task, how to brief, how to structure and specify a project, how to verify what the machine wrote, how to chain, automate and delegate the work, and what it costs. Principles stated for any assistant, worked on Claude with every product fact dated, and on one real project, Academy, with its own error counts. Ten core units in three parts and three deep dives.

What you will be able to do

By the end of the course you can say what the model saw when an output fails; brief a task so that the first output is usable and keep a project structure that does not rot; verify AI-written work by sample before you trust it; and decide what to hand to an agent, with one real project of your own specified and running.

  • Outcome 1 — a working model of the tool and a routing table with cost (BW1, BW2, BW10).
  • Outcome 2 — a specified project that produces usable first outputs (BW3, BW4, BW5, BW8; DD-BW1 sets it beside the documented methods).
  • Outcome 3 — a verification protocol, a pipeline and an agent rule, run on your own project (BW6, BW7, BW9; DD-BW2 and DD-BW3).

Every unit is sourced to peer-reviewed work, vendor documentation or a practitioner's own words, grades each method by its evidence, dates every product fact, says what holds only for one tool, and ends with a deliverable built in full on a real project and then as a template.

    Frame

    A method, for people who do not need to be taught how to think

    Courses on AI tools mostly teach prompts or teach code. The reader this course is for needs neither first: they reason and write well already, and what they lack is a method for a whole project: what the model can see, how to brief it, where a project's knowledge should live, how to specify the work so one context can finish a piece of it, and how to check the result without reading every line. The course teaches that method on the reader's own project, states each principle so that it holds for any current assistant, and works the detail on Claude, where the evidence was verified, with every product fact dated.

    Pick

    Method before tools; one real project as the case, with its error counts.

    • Principles outlive a model lineup; dated appendices carry the rest.
    • A real record of errors teaches verification better than advice.
    • The reader builds their own project unit by unit.

    Override if: most early readers turn out to use another assistant; then each unit's section on other tools is widened into a parallel worked example.

    • Reader: an experienced professional who reasons and writes well, uses a chat assistant with no method, may not write code, and brings one real project. Every brief says so.
    • Case: Academy, a real library of courses built by one person with Claude. Its facts come only from the case file, generator/data/building-with-ai/case-academy.md, with private details removed, and it is always labelled as one anecdote.
    • Tool stance: each unit ends its body with a section on other tools, saying what is specific to Claude and where the equivalent is documented.
    • Where the evidence pushes back: most guidance in this field is practice or opinion, studies test models that are already superseded, and a vendor's documentation is a vendor's. The course grades every method and dates every product fact.
