BUILDING WITH AI · PUBLIC · BRIEFS

Building with AI: the briefs

One brief per unit, self-contained: the shared preamble (the reader, the source-material rule, the tool stance, the Academy case, the course goal, the sourcing standard, the tone) is baked into every open brief, followed by the unit's Aim, Takes and Gives lines. Written units show their note instead. The course itself is read in Course BW.

Mental model

not written

The context window, tokens as cost and attention, what persists and what does not, trained against retrieved knowledge, why the same prompt answers differently.

Takes B1 as source material. Gives the vocabulary every later unit uses; the diagnosis table BW3, BW4 and BW6 point back to.

Shipsthe five-minute model + a table for diagnosing a failed output

Opus brief
Write one unit of a self-study course in the long-form report format of the Learning Project's markdown courses: a long-form report in Markdown. Length is whatever the topic needs (2,000–6,000 words); do not pad and do not cut to a target. Reader: an experienced professional who already reasons and writes well (an analyst, a designer, a product manager, a researcher, a lawyer, a founder), uses a chat assistant most days with no method, may not write code, and brings one real project to build through the course (a report, a course, a body of research, a small tool, a content operation); reads in order (each unit assumes the ones before it), reads on a phone, checks the citations, and wants genuine depth rather than a primer. Address the reader as 'you'. This course is written to be shared with anyone; it is an exception to the Learning Project's single-reader rule (decided 11 Nov 2026). Source material: the Takes line names the units of a private course this unit is adapted from. Take their evidence and their structure where they serve this reader; re-verify every claim you carry over against its source before you use it; carry over nothing about that course's own reader (no name, employer, city, health detail, personal project, tool of his own making, handle or household detail appears here); and never refer to the private course or its units, which a reader of this course cannot open. Point only to units of this course, by code (BW4, DD-BW1). Tool stance: state each principle so that it holds for any current assistant; work the detail on Claude (claude.ai and Claude Code), where the source evidence was verified; date every product fact (a limit, a price, a feature, a model name) to the day it was read on the vendor's own page; and end the body, before the deliverable, with a short section 'On other tools' that says which of the unit's claims are specific to Claude and where the equivalent is documented for ChatGPT and for Gemini, cited to the vendor's own documentation, or says that no equivalent was found. Running case: Academy (real), a library of self-study courses built by one person with Claude in October and November 2026, each unit written by one model from a brief, checked by a fresh context and audited by sample. Facts about Academy come only from generator/data/building-with-ai/case-academy.md, cited as 'Academy case file' with its section; never infer a fact about the case that the file does not hold (if the unit needs one, list it in the notes as a need). Label the case '(Academy case)' on its first use in the unit. One project is an anecdote: label it so, and never let it be the only support for a general claim. Deliverables are built twice: in full on the case (or, where the brief says so, on a neutral example with its invented content labelled), then as a template the reader fills for their own project, each blank saying what goes in it. Tone: direct, no flattery, no filler, no motivational language, no 'not X but Y' constructions. Course goal: by the end of the course you can say what the model saw when an output fails; brief a task so that the first output is usable and keep a project structure that does not rot; verify AI-written work by sample before you trust it; and decide what to hand to an agent, with one real project of your own specified and running. Sourcing standard: every claim that carries a number, a mechanism or a recommendation is sourced where it is made; acceptable sources are peer-reviewed work and preprints (a preprint labelled as one), vendor documentation and statements (labelled as vendor, dated), named practitioners' own posts, talks and repositories (labelled as practice), and independent benchmarks with their version and date; each method or practice carries an evidence grade: measured, documented practice, or stated opinion; no uncited figures; no invented names, studies, quotations or numbers; if a claim cannot be sourced, say so and leave it out of the deliverable. A unit that reads as advice without evidence is a failed unit. Links are given as links, so the course bibliography can collect them. Figures: where a section's content has a shape, declare it as a fenced viz block of a kind kit/viz.py draws (one to four a unit is a guide; none where nothing has a shape); a figure adds no fact, and its caption says what it shows and where its data comes from. Where the unit depends on current facts (model lineups, prices, plan limits, product features), search before writing and date the facts. End with the unit's deliverable, built out in full, not described.

Aim: say what the model could see when an output fails, and what to change
Takes: B1 as source material.
Gives: the vocabulary every later unit uses; the diagnosis table BW3, BW4 and BW6 point back to.

Unit BW1, Mental model. Search before writing. Cover: the context window as the one thing you control; tokens as cost and as attention; what persists inside a chat and across chats, and what a memory feature changes (dated); knowledge from training, knowledge retrieved and what is in the conversation; the training cutoff; why the same prompt gives different answers; what a system prompt, a project instruction and a user message each do; fabrication as a consequence of how a model produces text, not an occasional fault; the layers a current assistant offers (chat, projects, memory, tools and connectors, skills, agents) as a map the later units fill in. Deliverable: a model of how an assistant works that you could say to a colleague in five minutes, as plain prose; and a diagnosis table (the symptom, its likely cause in the model, what to change, the unit that treats it).

Unit BW2, Choosing the model and the effort. Search before writing. Cover: cost per task rather than per token; the tiers vendors offer (small and fast, mid, large) as a pattern that outlives any lineup; what effort or thinking settings do, from the vendors' documentation; where quality differences show and where they do not, with benchmark evidence and its limits (who runs it, which version, which date); self-preference, and why a check is better run by a different model or a fresh context; a subscription against the API; and a rule for re-deciding when the lineup changes.
