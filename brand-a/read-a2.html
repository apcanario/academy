01Who the audience is

The brief defines the audience by four traits: aged 30 to 45, employed, in at least one serious pursuit, and a restless brain. No survey measures that population as a whole. What exists is data on each trait separately, mostly American, mostly vendor-published. The profile at the end is assembled from those parts, and the joins are labelled as such.

Age. In 2026 a 30-to-45-year-old was born between 1981 and 1996, which is the millennial cohort as PYMNTS defines it (1981–1996) plus part of 1980, the youngest Gen X year (PYMNTS: 1965–1980) (PYMNTS Intelligence, Mar 2025; the arithmetic is mine). Where a study reports by generation, the millennial row is the one that describes his audience.

Employed. No source used here describes the audience by employment; the trait is the brief's definition and is carried unsourced.

The serious pursuit. Of his pursuits, running is the only one with large-sample demographic data.

  • RunRepeat's analysis of 107.9 million race results from more than 70,000 events (1986–2018) found the average half-marathon finisher's age rose from 37.5 to 39, and the average runner across distances was 39.3 in 2018 (RunRepeat, State of Running, published Jul 2019, updated Mar 2024). The data stops in 2018. On that evidence the half marathon he is training for draws a field centred inside his age band.
  • Strava reports more than 180 million users in more than 185 countries (Strava Year in Sport 2025 release, 3 Dec 2025). That is a company figure. Strava is a likely place to find the running slice of the audience (my inference); it is not a publishing channel and gets no hours here.

Karate has no participant or audience demographics in any source found. The proxies:

  • The top-ranked channel on FeedSpot's karate list, Jesse Enkamp, had 1.67 million YouTube subscribers on 27 October 2026 and gained 20,000 between 29 September and 26 October (vidIQ, updated 28 Oct 2026). CreatorDB describes his audience in prose as "predominantly adult males across the US, UK, and beyond"; its demographic charts are behind a paywall and the page gives no percentages (CreatorDB, 25 Jul 2026). That is vendor narrative without figures.
  • The World Karate Federation had 818,000 YouTube subscribers in January 2026, when it said all its major competitions would be available on YouTube "from now on" (FrancsJeux, 8 Jan 2026); FeedSpot listed it at 834K on 2 July 2026.
  • Below those two the category thins fast. FeedSpot's karate list, ranked by popularity, which FeedSpot says weighs subscribers, video views, uploads, and quality and consistency, had TEAM KI at 117K, Shotokan Karate at 52.3K, and positions five to ten between 38.9K and 16.1K (FeedSpot, updated 2 Jul 2026).

The history course and the weight loss have no audience data in this unit. This unit found no source that sizes an audience for a documented, self-taught history pursuit or for a weight-loss log; both are unsourced and stay out of the deliverable as audience claims.

The restless brain. This trait has no measure. One slice of it does. A meta-analysis of 40 articles from 30 countries put the prevalence of persistent adult ADHD at 2.58% and of symptomatic adult ADHD at 6.76%, adjusted to the 2020 world population structure (Song et al., Journal of Global Health, 11 Feb 2021). An ADHD audience on YouTube is large and already served: How to ADHD had 1.9 million subscribers and 12 of the 75 channels FeedSpot shows had more than 100,000 (FeedSpot, updated 2 Jul 2026). ADHD Chatter, Alex Partridge's interview podcast (host named on FeedSpot), showed 408,000 subscribers, about 83,300 average views a video and about 1.2 uploads a week on OutlierKit (OutlierKit, 21 Sep 2026); FeedSpot shows the same channel at 318K, a gap between trackers that recurs across every vendor count in this unit.

The restless brain beyond ADHD, the person who serially takes up pursuits, is a positioning claim. A1 owns that claim. The channel's own data is the only evidence that can test it: the signal would be the same viewers returning across journeys, which YouTube's Regular-viewer segment can show only from the second half of year one (A9 §2).

Language and geography. He publishes in English. Portugal is a small, saturated market: in late 2025 YouTube's ad reach was 7.59 million, Instagram's 6.35 million, LinkedIn's 6.10 million registered members and TikTok's 4.11 million adults (DataReportal, Digital 2026: Portugal, 8 Nov 2025). DataReportal warns these are advertising-reach figures and should not be read as monthly active users. Portuguese running creators already write for an English audience: in Modash's top 20 Portuguese running profiles on Instagram (of 172 tracked), the four largest have English bios, and the largest with a Portuguese bio has 14.6K followers (Modash, 21 Oct 2026; the reading of bios is mine). The Portuguese-language pool is Brazil, where YouTube's ad reach was 150 million in late 2025 (DataReportal, Digital 2026: Brazil, 8 Nov 2025). Reaching it would take a Brazilian-Portuguese track, which is a second production line (my inference). Portuguese stays as texture in an English channel.

Two limits. The figures are American, and they measure whether someone ever uses a platform, which says nothing about time spent or whether they would find him there.

YouTube reaches nine in ten of the age band and nine in ten college graduates. It is also where this age band listens to long-form talk. Edison Research found 68% of Americans aged 35 to 54 consumed a podcast in the past month in 2026, up from 51% in 2023, though from 2025 the measure counts watching as well as listening (Edison Research, The Podcast Consumer 2026, slides 13–14). Among weekly podcast consumers, YouTube was the service used most often, at 32% in Q1 2025 against 25% for Spotify (Edison Research, Top 10 Findings of 2025, slide 9); trade press reports the 2026 figure as 37% (Radio Ink, 5 Jun 2026), which appears to match slide 30 of Edison's 2026 deck (service used most often, Q1 2026), though the slide's labels could not be confirmed. Patreon's company-sponsored survey of 2,002 US fans found 32% of what fans consume on YouTube comes from following or subscription feeds (Patreon, State of Create, fieldwork Aug 2024). The other two thirds comes from outside the following feed, which is the only route by which a new channel gets seen (mechanics in A6; the subscriber-feed evidence in A9 §1).

Instagram reaches 64% of the age band. Patreon's survey puts the share of Instagram consumption from following feeds at 25% (Patreon, State of Create). Adam Mosseri named watch time, likes and sends as the three signals that matter most for ranking, with likes "slightly more important for connected content" and sends slightly more important for "unconnected content", which Social Media Today glosses as reaching people who do not follow the account (Social Media Today, 22 Jan 2025). Trial Reels, launched 10 December 2024, are "shown to non-followers first" (Instagram, 10 Dec 2024); a Meta spokesperson told Lia Haberman they were available to professional accounts (Haberman, 10 Dec 2024). Instagram's own announcement states no follower minimum.

What a small account gets is modest. Socialinsider's sample of 7,032,675 posts from 131,955 business accounts under 5,000 followers found 580 average views per Reel and 993 per carousel in the 1K–5K tier, and annual follower growth in that tier falling from 38% in 2024 to 22% in 2025 (Socialinsider, Instagram benchmarks, 20 Feb 2026). Those are brand accounts; Metricool and HypeAuditor's study of about 700 million posts from 28 million influencer accounts (January–June 2025) found influencer posts draw about three times the likes and comments of brand accounts (Metricool, press release, 14 Oct 2025), so Socialinsider's numbers are a floor for a person. Both are vendor data.

Instagram is also where creator commerce makes contact. Click Analytic found 39% of Instagram creators list a public email address, against 4.6% of TikTok creators; its TikTok base is 15.8 million accounts with at least 10,000 followers, full-year 2025, against 5.9 million Instagram accounts, for which the page gives no follower threshold or period (vendor data) (Click Analytic, 2026 edition). Platform money in Portugal is thin: Instagram listed Portugal for Subscriptions in a September 2023 update (Instagram, Subscriptions, updated 20 Sep 2023), while the 33 countries Meta listed for Gifts in November 2023 do not include it (Meta for Creators, 6 Nov 2023). No newer official page was found for either.

TikTok reaches 45% of the age band and 4.11 million Portuguese adults. Its following feeds carry 38% of what fans watch (Patreon, State of Create). Small brand accounts under 5,000 followers averaged 350 views a video on TikTok, against 625 on Reels and 15,160 on YouTube Shorts, across 145,444 accounts from January 2025 to June 2026; accounts under 1,000 followers were excluded (Socialinsider, updated 21 Oct 2026). Vendor data, brand accounts. Only 2.7% of TikTok creators with 10,000 or more followers list a YouTube account (Click Analytic), and no source found measures how many TikTok viewers go on to a creator's YouTube channel. Whether Portugal is in TikTok's Creator Rewards Program is unconfirmed: TikTok's own eligibility page could not be read, and two aggregator pages from July 2026 give conflicting country lists, neither of which includes Portugal (Quasa, 27 Jul 2026; TT Calculator, 9 Jul 2026).

LinkedIn reaches 37% of the age band and 51% of college graduates, the highest education skew in Pew's table. In Metricool's study (vendor data) of 673,658 posts from 63,108 accounts, personal profiles had 2.60% engagement against 1.60% for company pages, carousels averaged 1,451 impressions a post against 606 for video, and posts with links on personal profiles got 27% fewer impressions (Metricool, LinkedIn study, 16 Apr 2026). Buffer's key findings put LinkedIn's median engagement at about 6.2%, the highest of the seven networks it ranks by engagement rate (Buffer measured ten; three use other metrics), though its own body text gives about 6.1%, it measures only posts published through Buffer, and platforms define engagement differently (Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026, 5 Mar 2026). For a senior director the open question is the employer. Sonia Kang, quoted on brands built alongside a job, put the line this way: an Etsy side gig "is probably much more acceptable to corporations than something like, 'This is me, and here's my view'" (The Globe and Mail; page undated). Contract terms are A8's.

Email is the channel where a follower count means delivered attention. beehiiv reports open rates rising from 37.98% in 2024 (37.67% in its own comparison graphic) to 41.24% in 2025 across a base of 65,000-plus newsletters and 28 billion emails (beehiiv, State of Newsletters 2026, 6 Jan 2026). That is platform data. It is the only figure in this unit that measures the share of an audience reached per item sent.

03What converts them

Three conversions matter, and they rest on evidence of very different quality.

Stranger to viewer. On YouTube this is per-video recommendation: impressions, click-through and view duration, judged video by video. A6 explains the mechanics; A9 §1 and §2 give the base rates. Nothing about the audience profile changes it. The audience converts on a title and a thumbnail that promise their pursuit, delivered by a video that holds them.

Viewer to owned list. No source found measures the share of YouTube viewers or Instagram followers who join a creator's email list. That rate is unsourced, and the allocation plans around no number for it. What is documented is that newsletter platforms supply subscribers from inside their own networks. Substack said recommendations and its app drove 50% of all new subscriptions and 25% of new paid subscriptions (On Substack, 22 Feb 2024); in October 2024 it said the app drove 3 million subscriptions in the past month and recommendations 2 million, and that app discovery was generating "about the same number of subscriptions as all social media sources combined" (On Substack, 31 Oct 2024). beehiiv says newsletters using its Recommendations "grow 2.75x faster, and those that recommend others are 32x more likely to be recommended back" (beehiiv, 6 Jan 2026). Both are platform claims without published method. The practical consequence: for a list that starts at zero, the platform's network is the only measured source of subscribers, and a call to action inside an episode is an unmeasured second one.

Follower or subscriber to buyer. The rates (how many of a list pay, how many followers ever buy) are A9 §7, and A7 applies them to Shōmen. What this unit adds is what this audience is documented to pay for.

  • They buy on creator recommendation, at least occasionally. PYMNTS surveyed 3,196 US consumers, weighted to the US adult population (fieldwork 10–15 January; the page gives no year; published March 2025): 72% of millennials and 58% of Gen X made at least one influencer-recommended purchase in a year, and 12% of all consumers did so more than six times (PYMNTS Intelligence, Mar 2025). An older Pew survey (6,034 US adults, July 2022) found 33% of social media users aged 30 to 49 had bought something after seeing an influencer post about it (Pew Research Center, 21 Nov 2022). Both are self-report.
  • They spend on the gear their pursuit needs. Running USA's 2024 Global Runner Survey (more than 7,400 runners, 94% American, June–July 2024, sponsored by Active) found 24.9% spend more than $400 a year on running shoes and 24.7% spend $301–400, so about half spend more than $300 (my sum); on apparel 29% spend $100–200 and 21% $201–300 (SGB Media, 21 Oct 2024). This is a reporter's summary of a sponsored survey. No equivalent exists for karate equipment.
  • They say they would pay for long-form. Patreon's fans were more likely to pay for long-form content (49%) than short-form (29%) (Patreon, State of Create). Company-sponsored, US-only, a stated preference.
  • Structured, educational products are where creators report growth. Kajabi's survey of 1,717 creators (December 2024 to January 2025) reported digital-download sales up 20% and educational content up 14%, against brand deals down 52%, affiliate income down 36% and platform payouts down 33% (Net Influencer, 17 Apr 2025, reporting Kajabi's 2025 State of Creator Commerce; the three declines also in Silicon UK, 17 Apr 2025). These are creators reporting their own revenue changes on a platform that sells course tools; it says nothing about who the buyers were.
  • Affiliate commission on gear is small per sale. Amazon.es, the Amazon store that serves Portugal, pays 4.0% in Deportes y Fitness, 6.0% on clothing (Ropa y Accesorios) and on shoes (Zapatos, Bolsos y Carteras) and 5.0% on books (Amazon.es Associates fee table; no effective date shown). A €120 pair of running shoes earns €7.20 at 6% (my arithmetic). Whether a Portuguese resident can join the Amazon.es programme was not confirmed.

Why an audience trusts one creator enough to buy has peer-reviewed evidence from a different population: in a 2026 cross-sectional survey of 407 Indonesian women who follow beauty influencers, perceived expertise and trustworthiness predicted the parasocial bond that predicted purchase intention, while attractiveness did not predict the bond but was directly related to purchase intention (Muhmin, Syah and Anggraeni, Frontiers in Communication, 2026). A4 treats it with the disclosure policy.

Put together, the sourced picture is that runners spend on the equipment running consumes (one sponsored, mostly US survey) and that this audience, on weaker evidence, would pay for long-form or structured guidance from someone it trusts. Every source behind that sentence is American or global, and none is Portuguese. The share of his own audience that will pay for anything is unknown until an offer runs and is measured per A9's checkpoints.

04Why YouTube long-form compounds

The mechanism is stated by YouTube. Its help centre says "It's common for viewers to start showing more interest in old videos" and that "Many viewers don't watch videos in chronological order or decide what to watch based on when a video was published"; its recommendation system "aims to deliver the right videos to the right viewers, regardless of when that video was uploaded" (YouTube Help, performance FAQ, answer 141805). A separate page says "publish time is not known to impact a video's long-term performance" (YouTube Help, YouTube's recommendation system, answer 16533387). PPC Land's report of Todd Beaupré's September 2026 interview adds, in its own paraphrase, that some videos "accumulate the bulk of their audience a month or more after publication" (PPC Land, 2 Sep 2026). A video is a standing entry in the recommendation pool for as long as it satisfies viewers. A catalogue of 26 episodes a year is 26 entries that keep drawing impressions.

The vendor data shows the same shape. In vidIQ's 2025 cohorts, median 30-day views on long-form rose from about 180 on the first upload to about 272 on the tenth across 137,355 channels, while the Shorts cohort of 367,243 channels went from about 1,362 to about 1,344, which vidIQ describes as "no incremental growth, in fact a slight decline over time" (vidIQ, First 10 Videos, 28 Jul 2026). The sample excludes channels that did not publish ten same-format videos within their first 90 days, so it is a cohort posting about weekly or faster (he reaches ten uploads in about 18 weeks; my arithmetic), and it is observational. A practitioner model goes further: Humble&Brag estimates that by month six, with 24 to 26 videos, back-catalogue views start to exceed the views on new uploads, but its own assumptions are one video a week and about 450 first-week views each (Humble&Brag, 9 Mar 2026). That is a model, without a measured dataset, and at his cadence video 24 arrives around month 11 and video 26 at month 12 (my arithmetic).

For Instagram and TikTok no source found measures how a post's views accumulate over months. Socialinsider and Buffer report per-post and per-week figures. Whether a Reel or a TikTok compounds is therefore unsourced, and the allocation treats both as producing value in the week they are posted.

The money favours long-form per view. YouTube pays creators 55% of net revenue from Watch Page ads and 45% of the revenue allocated to them from Shorts Feed ads (YouTube Help, YouTube partner earnings overview, answer 72902). Alphabet's chief executive said on the Q3 2025 earnings call (29 October 2025) that "In the U.S., Shorts now earn more revenue per watch hour than traditional in-stream on YouTube" (PPC Land, 6 Nov 2025, reporting the call). That is YouTube's revenue per hour watched. A Short is watched for seconds and an episode for minutes, so even at equal revenue per hour, revenue per view stays far higher on long-form (my inference; no source found gives creator revenue per view for the two formats side by side). Long-form watch hours are also what count toward the Partner Program's watch-hour route; the thresholds, the February 2027 change and the RPM figures for his niches are in A9 §6.

Cadence: bi-weekly sits at the low edge of the best-performing band. AIR Media-Tech's 12-month snapshot of 18,000 English-language channels in 11 niches (US, UK, Canada, Australia) found that in Health & Fitness the median channel posting 2–5 videos a month had 172,000 subscribers (82 channels) against 77,700 for channels posting 10–20 (63 channels); in Education the 2–5 band also peaked, at 116,000. Channels on a predictable schedule got more views per subscriber than channels posting as many videos on random days (AIR Media-Tech, 8 Jun 2026). AIR calls this correlational, and its channels are far larger than his. Bi-weekly is about 2.2 long-form videos a month (my arithmetic), inside the band at its bottom edge.

Shorts as the second format: the two datasets disagree at his volume. AIR's Shorts study (18,000 channels, March 2025 to March 2026, at least 40 videos) found channels mixing Shorts had more median subscribers than long-form-only channels in Fitness (41% more), Business (39%) and Education (37% at smaller sizes), put "the optimal Shorts ratio" at 25% to 40% of total uploads, and found Shorts "crowding out" long-form above 55% (AIR Media-Tech, 28 May 2026). Metricool's 799,718 videos from 71,177 accounts found channels posting around five Shorts a month averaged 8,789 views per long-form video, about 46% above the global average of 5,985, and that at 35 or more Shorts a month performance fell back to roughly the level of channels posting none (Metricool, YouTube Trends 2026, 29 Jul 2026). Both are vendor and correlational. At 2.2 episodes a month, AIR's 25–40% share means 0.7 to 1.4 Shorts a month, while Metricool's peak sits near five (my arithmetic). One Short per episode gives two a month and a 50% share, under AIR's 55% line and between the two findings. That is the allocation's choice, and neither dataset tested it. It also means the "2–3 Shorts per episode" in A3's brief would put Shorts at 67–75% of uploads, above AIR's crowding line (my arithmetic); A3 should take the one-Short figure from here.

The search route has weakened. In Pew's tracking of 900 US adults' browsing in March 2025, users who saw a Google AI summary clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits against 15% without one, and clicked a link inside the summary in 1% (Pew Research Center, 22 Jul 2025). Ahrefs, comparing 300,000 keywords between December 2023 and December 2025, found an AI Overview correlates with a 58% lower average click-through rate for the top-ranked page (Ahrefs, 4 Feb 2026); Seer Interactive's 3,119 search terms across 42 client organisations show organic click-through on AI Overview queries falling from 1.76% to 0.61% between June 2024 and September 2025 (Seer Interactive, 4 Nov 2025). Ahrefs and Seer are vendor data. A written archive will be found through the newsletter's network and through links from the videos far more than through search.

The arithmetic for the fee: Substack's 10% costs more than Ghost's $348 a year (12 × $29) once paid revenue passes $3,480 a year (my arithmetic, at Ghost's 1,000-member tier; processor fees apply to both and are left out). Until a paid offer exists the fee is zero.

The vault stays the private master record; the public archive is what gets published from it. How the two stay in step is B4's question.

06What the hours buy

The budget is 5 to 7 hours a week, 10 to 14 a fortnight. Most components have no measured time cost; where one exists it is cited, and the rest are the allocation's own estimates, which the hours log in A9's checkpoints (read 2) will correct.

  • Long-form editing runs "30–60 minutes per finished minute" of video by a vendor rule of thumb with no stated method (Tasty Edits, updated 17 Sep 2026). A 10-minute episode is 5 to 10 hours of editing; a 12-minute episode is 6 to 12. At the top of that range the edit alone fills a fortnight. The allocation therefore sets the episode at about 10 minutes and plans on the low end, 5 hours. A5's brief aims lower (a 12-minute episode in under 4 hours); the plan keeps 5 until that workflow is in use.
  • A short weekly email can be written in 45 minutes with a four-step formula, by Justin Welsh's own account, picking topics from his best-performing social posts (Justin Welsh, 6 Apr 2024). That is an anecdote from a full-time creator; here the episode supplies the topic.
  • Instagram frequency matters less than consistency. Buffer's fixed-effects analysis of 2.1 million posts from 102,000 accounts found weekly follower growth of 0.12% at 1–2 posts a week and 0.26% at 3–5, each relative to weeks with no posts (Buffer, 13 Aug 2025). Vendor data, feed posts only. Two posts a week cut from the episode's footage sits in the lower band at the lowest cost.
  • Replies to comments are on YouTube's own list of ways to grow Regular viewers, alongside publishing regularly and making content "more consistent and similar in topic or format" (YouTube Help, answer 13615784). The allocation gives them fixed slots, which is also the distancing practice A9 §8 takes from the ASQ study.
  • The cadence has to survive the worst week. Amy Suto's rule is to pick a cadence you can hit "on your worst week, not your best one" (Amy Suto, 4 Jul 2026); that is an anecdote. AIR's association between going dark for 14 or more days three or more times a year and fewer views per video is in A9 §8. Both point to the same cut order: shrink the derivatives and the episode, hold the fortnightly date.

08Deliverable 2Platform allocation, 5–7 hours a week

One page, for one long-form episode every two weeks from upload one. Hours are per fortnight, then per week. Cited costs are marked; the rest are this allocation's estimates, tested by the hours log at every quarterly checkpoint (A9, Deliverable 2, read 2).

Pick: YouTube long-form is the home platform.

  • Reaches 91% of US 30–49s; old videos keep being recommended.
  • Long-form views grew across first ten uploads; Shorts stayed flat.
  • 55% revenue share; long-form hours feed Partner Program eligibility.

Override if: A9's stop rule fires (fewer than 3 uploads in two consecutive quarters); then the system is rebuilt before any platform change.

Pick: Substack holds the written archive and the email.

  • List starts at zero; Substack says its network drove half of new subscriptions platform-wide (Feb 2024).
  • Free until a paid offer exists.
  • List exports as CSV, so the choice is reversible.

Override if: paid revenue passes about $3,480 a year while the list is within Ghost's 1,000-member tier (above it Ghost costs more, so recompute), or Substack's terms change on fees or export.
