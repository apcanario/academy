01Creator income for an employee: when he must register, and what is taxed

Registration comes before the income. The Código do IRS requires that "Antes de iniciar alguma atividade suscetível de produzir rendimentos da categoria B", the taxpayer files a declaração de início de atividade (CIRS art. 112.º, n.º 1, AT site). The AT's own leaflet (March 2026) says the same in plain words: an individual who intends to carry on a business or professional activity files it "antes de começar a trabalhar por conta própria", online on the Portal das Finanças, choosing activity codes from "lista de atividades do art.º 151.º do CIRS" (AT, Início de Atividade, March 2026). Being employed does not change this: the article applies to anyone whose activity can produce category B income, and the leaflet names no exception for employees. Recibos verdes are the invoices issued once the activity is open.

The one-off exception. A single payment that will not repeat can be invoiced as an ato isolado without opening an activity. The Ordem dos Contabilistas Certificados reads the IRS concept as income that does not come from a foreseeable or repeated practice ("não resultem de uma prática previsível ou reiterada", CIRS art. 3.º, n.º 3), and concludes that three payments for continuing work are "difícil" to treat as one act (OCC, technical opinion PT23308, 26 Aug 2019; professional body, secondary to the code); a later opinion holds that services provided with continuity, as part of an economic activity, call for the activity to be opened rather than an ato isolado (OCC, technical opinion PT27834, December 2023, published 29 Jan 2024). Both opinions applied an IVA ceiling of €25,000 to an ato isolado, and that ceiling no longer holds: from 1 July 2025 an ato isolado needs no declaração de início de atividade for IVA "independentemente do respetivo valor" (AT, Ofício-Circulado 25066, 15 Apr 2025, on Decreto-Lei 49/2025; the AT leaflet of March 2026 says the same), and its value counts toward the year's turnover for the art. 53.º threshold (AT, Ofício-Circulado 25062, 26 Mar 2025). The plan in A2, A3 and A7 is recurring by design: a weekly email, a paid tier from September 2027 if the list allows (A7, table B1, a8), and two Shōmen drops a year. Recurring income fails the ato-isolado test as the OCC reads it, so the first recurring payment needs the activity opened first.

Category B is taxed together with the salary. IRS taxable income "resulta do englobamento dos rendimentos das várias categorias auferidos em cada ano" (CIRS art. 22.º, n.º 1, AT site). Creator income is added to employment income and taxed at his marginal rate. This unit does not state the 2026 rate brackets; they were not opened.

Simplified regime and coefficients. The regime simplificado applies while category B gross income did not exceed €200,000 in the previous year, and the taxpayer may opt for organised accounts instead (CIRS art. 28.º, n.os 2 and 3, AT site). Under it, taxable income is gross income multiplied by a coefficient (CIRS art. 31.º, n.º 1, AT site, read 2 Nov 2026):

Coefficient What it applies to (art. 31.º, n.º 1) Where his streams might fall
0.15 Sales of goods and products Shōmen merch, if sold under his own activity
0.35 Services not covered by another item The AT treats code 1519, "Outros prestadores de serviços", as the residual code at 0.35 (AT, ficha doutrinária, proc. 3870/2018, 21 Dec 2018)
0.75 Professional activities in the art. 151.º table Applies only if his code is on that table
0.95 Among others, licensing of intellectual property A question for ad revenue and paid subscriptions

No AT page or binding ruling found on 2 November 2026 says which code or coefficient applies to YouTube ad revenue, a paid newsletter or a brand deal. That choice is the accountant's.

Two rules in the same article bear on him. Under n.º 13, the 0.75 and 0.35 coefficients assume expenses: where 15% of gross service income exceeds the listed deductible items, "a diferença positiva entre 15 % dos rendimentos brutos das prestações de serviços" is added back to taxable income. Under n.º 10, the start-of-activity reductions of 50% and 25% to those coefficients require that the taxpayer earn no category A or H income in those periods; as an employee he earns category A, so on the text the reduction does not reach him (my reading; the accountant confirms).

Withholding. A category B earner below the IVA exemption threshold can opt out of withholding by writing on the receipt "Sem retenção, nos termos do n.º 1 do artigo 101.º-B do Código do IRS"; the option ends the month after the threshold is reached (CIRS art. 101.º-B, AT site).

Gifts from brands. In June 2026 two news outlets reported an AT alert to influencers that income in kind must be declared (ECO, 28 Jun 2026; Pplware, 24 Jun 2026); neither links an AT document, and none was found. ECO quotes a software vendor's view that a product is taxed through "a contrapartida paga pela divulgação desse produto". Both are secondary; the treatment of a gifted product is the accountant's question, and until it is answered his rule is to accept no product in exchange for a mention.

Substack lets writers configure Stripe Tax so that "taxes correctly calculated for subscribers in relevant tax jurisdictions" (Substack, 6 Feb 2023); the post does not say whether Substack itself collects or remits VAT. How IVA applies to each foreign payer, and whether any registration in the EU VAT system is needed for income received, was not settled by any primary source found. Open, for the accountant.

02IVA: the 2026 exemption threshold

The regime de isenção covers taxpayers who "não tenham atingido, no ano civil anterior, um volume de negócios anual em território nacional superior a 15 000 €" (CIVA art. 53.º, n.º 1, AT site, as amended by Decreto-Lei 35/2025, read 2 Nov 2026). An exempt taxpayer charges no IVA and deducts none. A new activity uses its estimated turnover for the current year (n.º 5), so the declaração de início sets the regime from his own estimate; the AT leaflet confirms the regime follows "em função dos elementos constantes da declaração de início de atividade".

The exit rule is on the AT's January 2026 newsletter: a taxpayer who passes €15,000 "em mais de 25%", that is, past €18,750, must charge IVA on the invoice that crosses €18,750, moves to the normal regime, and files a change declaration "no prazo de 15 dias úteis" (AT, Newsletter 39, January 2026). The running rule is in the code itself: the exemption ends when "No ano civil em curso, o referido limiar de isenção for excedido em mais de 25 %", with IVA due from that moment and the change declaration within 15 working days (CIVA art. 58.º, n.os 2, al. b), 4 and 5, AT site, as amended by Decreto-Lei 35/2025, read 2 Nov 2026). A year that passes €15,000 but stays at or under €18,750 also ends the exemption: the change declaration is due within 15 working days of the year's end, and IVA is charged on invoices from 1 January of the next year (AT leaflet, March 2026).

Whether any of them crosses €15,000 of his turnover depends on the exchange rate and on whose turnover Shōmen's sales are, and no filed unit states Shōmen's legal form, while row a16 of A7's table B1 says Shōmen's costs are in no filed unit. 03Segurança Social for an employee with independent income

The general rule. An independent worker contributes at "21,4 %" on relevant income, which for services is "70 % do valor total de prestação de serviços" and for goods 20%, measured over the three months before each quarterly declaration (Decreto-Lei 2/2018, arts. 162.º and 168.º of the Código dos Regimes Contributivos, Segurança Social site). First enrolment takes effect "no primeiro dia do 12.º mês posterior ao do início de atividade" (art. 145.º, same text). Quarterly declarations are due "até ao último dia dos meses de abril, julho, outubro e janeiro" (DGSS, Folha informativa, on the 2018 regime, undated).

The exemption for employees. An independent worker who is also employed is exempt when average monthly relevant income, averaged by quarter, is below four times the IAS, and all three of these hold: the two activities are for different employers with no relationship of domain or group between them; the employment brings mandatory cover by another regime for the same contingencies; and the monthly pay from employment is at least one IAS (gov.pt, Pedir a isenção do pagamento de contribuições para a Segurança Social, updated 3 Sep 2026). The OCC quotes the same three conditions from art. 157.º, including "prestados a entidades empregadoras distintas e que não tenham entre si uma relação de domínio ou de grupo" (OCC, 8 Feb 2019).

The 2026 figures. "O valor do IAS para o ano de 2026 é de € 537,13" (Portaria 480-A/2025/1, 30 Dec 2025, art. 2.º). Four times that is €2,148.52 of monthly relevant income; at the 70% rule for services that is about €3,069 of gross service income a month, averaged over the quarter (my arithmetic). The employment condition needs pay of at least €537.13 a month.

What is not settled. The gov.pt service is titled "Pedir a isenção", which suggests the exemption is requested; the page does not say whether it is ever applied automatically. Whether he still files quarterly declarations while exempt was not confirmed on any page opened. Both are for the accountant.

04The employment contract: what the law allows, what to check

Open question 1, from A2 and A4. A2's LinkedIn override says: "first check the employment contract (A8)" (A2, Deliverable 2), and A2 §2 ends its LinkedIn paragraph with "Contract terms are A8's". A4's pre-mortem, failure 8, "Work leaked into the record", gives as its why that "contract terms are A8's". This section sets what he checks, and in what order; it does not say what his contract contains, because no filed unit does.

The Code's default. Every Portuguese employment contract carries a duty of loyalty: "Guardar lealdade ao empregador, nomeadamente não negociando por conta própria ou alheia em concorrência com ele, nem divulgando informações referentes à sua organização, métodos de produção ou negócios" (Código do Trabalho, art. 128.º, n.º 1, al. f), as reproduced by informador.pt; the Diário da República consolidated text did not load). The Supremo Tribunal de Justiça, in February 2025, read that duty, quoting Maria do Rosário Palma Ramalho, as taking shape "no dever de não concorrência e no dever de sigilo", and held that a breach weighs more heavily with the worker's degree of responsibility and position in the hierarchy (STJ, proc. 14603/23.1T8LSB.L1.S1, 12 Feb 2025). The same judgment cites art. 129.º, n.º 1, al. k), which bars an employer from blocking another professional activity "salvo com base em fundamentos objetivos".

What a contract can add. The STJ upheld an exclusivity clause that allowed outside paid work only with prior written authorisation, holding such a clause valid when it is "indispensável à proteção de interesses legítimos" and proportionate, and that the Code requires no specific compensation for it (same judgment). A wider restriction than loyalty therefore exists only if the contract or a document it incorporates states one. No dataset was found on how common such clauses are in Portuguese contracts, so this unit says nothing about what a typical contract por tempo indeterminado contains. A post-employment non-compete is a separate pact in the Code; its article was not opened.

What the record already does. A4's disclosure policy keeps "My employer's internal matters and my colleagues" off the record (A4, Deliverable A, clause 2), and publishes the job only "as a constraint ('a full-time job'); never the employer's business" (clause 1). That covers the disclosure limb of art. 128.º, n.º 1, al. f) as written. The competition limb is untouched by a channel about running, karate and history, on the plain words; his employer's business is outside any filed unit, and the reading stays his.

The checklist (his rule; basis: art. 128.º, the STJ's 2025 reading of exclusivity and objective grounds):

  1. Any clause on exclusivity, accumulation of activities or outside work: does it require notice or prior written authorisation, and for what kinds of activity?
  2. Any clause on conflicts of interest, and any duty to declare outside income or shareholdings (Shōmen included).
  3. Any confidentiality clause, and any document it points to (a code of conduct, a handbook).
  4. Any social-media or public-communication policy: does it cover personal accounts, the use of his job title, or LinkedIn in particular?
  5. Any clause assigning to the employer rights in work he creates, and whether it reaches work made outside working hours or on his own equipment.
  6. Any post-employment non-compete.
  7. Working-time and availability terms that would collide with the fixed windows A3 sets (Saturday filming and editing, Tuesday packaging).

If items 1, 2 or 4 require notice or authorisation, he gives notice in writing before the first paid income and before the LinkedIn override fires, and keeps the reply. If the wording is unclear, a labour lawyer reads it. For LinkedIn the checklist matters most: A2 §2 reports higher engagement for personal profiles than for company pages there (Metricool, vendor data), and LinkedIn is the platform where his job title appears beside every post.

05Health data: is body weight "data concerning health", and what "manifestly made public" means

Open question 2, from A4. A4 §5 says that "whether body weight counts as health data, is a legal question for A8", and its unsure item 7 notes that the GDPR was read "in an unofficial reproduction". The text below comes from EUR-Lex, read 2 November 2026, where it loaded; where EUR-Lex returned only part of a provision, the as-adopted text on legislation.gov.uk (the UK National Archives' reproduction of the EU text) supplies the rest, and says so.

The definition. "'data concerning health' means personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person" (GDPR art. 4(15), EUR-Lex), continuing "including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about his or her health status" (as adopted, legislation.gov.uk). The Court of Justice in October 2024 held that order data from an online pharmacy were health data because they were "capable of revealing information about the health status of an identified or identifiable data subject", reading the category broadly (CJEU, C-21/23 Lindenapotheke, press release, 4 Oct 2024). The Article 29 Working Party's 2015 criteria for lifestyle data, as a law firm summarises them, include raw data that can be used "to draw conclusions about an individual's actual health status or health risks" (Hunton, 9 Feb 2015; secondary; the annex itself was not opened).

The answer for weight. No source found rules on body weight by name. On the definition and the Court's test, a weight figure attached to a named person, published in a journey framed as losing 15 kg, is capable of revealing information about health status; the cautious reading treats it as health data. That reading is mine, and a data-protection lawyer could confirm it. It supports A4's choice to publish weight change and never absolute weight (A4, Deliverable A, clauses 2 and 3).

What Art. 9(2)(e) means in practice. Art. 9(1) prohibits processing data concerning health; the prohibition does not apply where "processing relates to personal data which are manifestly made public by the data subject" (GDPR art. 9(2)(e), EUR-Lex). The Court of Justice has said what "manifestly" requires: the data subject must have "explicitly made the choice beforehand", with "full knowledge of the facts", to make the data "publicly accessible to an unlimited number of persons" (CJEU, C-252/21 Meta Platforms v Bundeskartellamt, 4 July 2023, FRA case summary). A public YouTube episode in which he states his weight change meets those words: he chose it, knowing it, for an unlimited audience. In practice, once a figure is in a public episode, the Art. 9(1) prohibition no longer applies to a third party processing that figure; that party still needs a lawful basis under Art. 6 and is bound by the rest of the Regulation, since Art. 9(2) lifts only the special-category prohibition (my reading of the text). What stays protected is what he never published: absolute weight, clinical measures and the rest of A4's clause 2. So A4's rule to "publish health facts narrowly" is the only control he keeps (my reading of the text and the judgment).

The other direction. The same law reaches his channel when it shows other people. The GDPR does not apply to processing "in the course of a purely personal or household activity" (art. 2(2)(c), EUR-Lex), and in Buivids the Court held that a video published on YouTube without access restrictions, reaching "an indefinite number of people", falls outside that exemption (SCL commentary on C-345/17, 15 Feb 2019; secondary). Training partners and the sponsored athletes in his footage are therefore personal data he processes, which §6 and §8 use.

06Commercial disclosure under Portuguese and EU law

Open question 3, from A4. A4's unsure item 9: "Portuguese and EU rules (the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive) were not opened and are A8's." A4 clause 7 and A7 §4 both deferred the Portuguese and EU rules here.

Portuguese advertising law. The Código da Publicidade defines advertising as "qualquer forma de comunicação feita por entidades de natureza pública ou privada" in the course of an economic or professional activity to promote goods or services (art. 3.º, n.º 1), requires that "A publicidade tem de ser inequivocamente identificada como tal, qualquer que seja o meio de difusão utilizado" (art. 8.º, n.º 1), and prohibits "imagens subliminares ou outros meios dissimuladores" (art. 9.º, n.º 1) (Código da Publicidade, municipal compilation incorporating amendments to 1997; the Diário da República consolidated text did not load, so later amendments to these articles were not checked). A post in which he shows or names Shōmen to sell it is communication in the course of an economic activity; on art. 3.º it is advertising, and art. 8.º requires it to be identifiable as such (my reading).

Unfair commercial practices. Decreto-Lei 57/2008 transposes Directive 2005/29/EC. It lists as misleading in every case "Utilizar um conteúdo editado nos meios de comunicação social para promover um bem ou serviço" where the trader paid and that is not clear (art. 8.º, al. m), formerly n)), and "Alegar falsamente ou dar a impressão de que o profissional não está a agir para fins" of his trade (al. z), formerly ab); both relettered by Decreto-Lei 109-G/2021, in force since 28 May 2022, read in a copy of the decree because the Diário da República page did not load); and treats as a misleading omission a practice "Em que o profissional não refere a intenção comercial da prática, se tal não se puder depreender do contexto" (art. 9.º, n.º 1, al. c)). ASAE is the general enforcer; for advertising, "é a Direção-Geral do Consumidor (DGC)", which may call on ASAE (art. 19.º, n.º 3, as amended by Decreto-Lei 109-G/2021) (Decreto-Lei 57/2008, consolidated text on the Banco de Portugal site, which carries amendments to Decreto-Lei 9/2021 only; art. 9.º, n.º 1, al. c) is unchanged by the later Decreto-Lei 109-G/2021). The Directive's matching provisions are Annex I point 11 (advertorial) and point 22, and art. 7(2): a practice is a misleading omission where a trader "fails to identify the commercial intent of the commercial practice if not already apparent from the context" (Directive 2005/29/EC, as adopted, Annex I; Chapter 2). The government's consumer guide, updated 11 May 2026, restates the omission rule (gov.pt, Práticas comerciais desleais).

How the DGC applies it to creators. The DGC's note on the Commission's Influencer Legal Hub says influencers with regular commercial activity are traders under EU consumer law and "são obrigados a fazer publicidade de maneira transparente" (DGC, Informação 34/2023, 16 Aug 2023). In February 2024 the DGC reported checking 366 Instagram messages from 20 influencers promoting 137 brands; 102 messages were irregular, the commercial relationship with 76 brands was not identified, and it opened administrative-offence proceedings (ECO, 9 Feb 2024; press report). The government's 2019 good-practice guide said "a identificação da publicidade é obrigatória em qualquer circunstância" (Governo, 29 Mar 2019); the Direção-Geral do Consumidor's guide for influencers and advertisers, Marketing de influência, read in an undated copy on the trade site Meios & Publicidade (filed under April 2019), says "Usar a indicação #PUB ou PUB adaptado à plataforma em questão", asks for the commercial relationship to be identified "no início da publicação", and lists #OFERTA for products supplied free (DGC guide, copy on meiosepublicidade.pt); it is guidance, not law. No legal text opened prescribes a hashtag or a set wording, and no ERC guidance on influencers was found.

What that means for the filed rules. The law asks that the commercial intent be identifiable, at once and without inference. A4 clause 7 ("Every mention of Shōmen says it is my brand"), A7's caption first line ("Shōmen is my brand"), A5's rule that a product in frame is a mention, and A7's use of YouTube's paid-promotion setting on any upload that shows Shōmen meet that on the words of art. 8.º and art. 9.º, n.º 1, al. c), as I read them. Instagram's own policy adds a tool for third-party deals: for branded content, "our branded content policies require that you use the paid partnership label to tag the brand" (Instagram Help, branded content); the page is silent on a creator's own brand. Any future deal with a brand other than Shōmen uses that label, YouTube's setting and a spoken line, as A4 clause 7 already sets.

07Image rights of training partners

Open question 4, from A5 and B3. A5's rule 3: "Training partners are asked in advance (B3's worked example for template 8 drafts that message; image rights are A8's)"; its checklist: "Ask every training partner who might be in frame, before the season, whether they agree to appear ... Image rights are A8's." B3's worked example for template 8 notes that "the image rights of training partners are on A8's brief".

The rule in Portuguese law. "O retrato de uma pessoa não pode ser exposto, reproduzido ou lançado no comércio sem o seu consentimento" (Código Civil, art. 79.º, n.º 1, as the Tribunal da Relação de Lisboa quotes it, proc. 127/12.6TAOER.L1, 26 Oct 2016). Consent is not needed, under n.º 2 as the same court sets it out, where notoriety or office justifies it, for police, justice, scientific, teaching or cultural purposes, or "quando a reprodução da imagem vier enquadrada na de lugares públicos, ou na de factos de interesse público ou que hajam decorrido publicamente". A session in a club is not a public place, and on a run on public roads the partner is the subject of the shot, where the exception speaks of an image framed within a public place; the channel will also carry Shōmen. Consent is the safe basis (my reading; a court could read the public-place exception more widely). The person pictured may authorise others to commercialise the image (Tribunal da Relação de Coimbra, proc. 2918/19.8T8VIS.C1, 16 Sep 2025, summary).

Consent can be withdrawn. A voluntary limitation of a personality right is "sempre revogável", with an "obrigação de indemnizar os prejuízos causados às legítimas expectativas da outra parte" (Código Civil, art. 81.º, n.º 2, as quoted by Cesa e Silva, Julgar Online, October 2021). Under the GDPR, where consent is the basis, "The data subject shall have the right to withdraw his or her consent at any time", and "It shall be as easy to withdraw as to give consent" (art. 7(3), EUR-Lex). Consent itself must be "freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous" (art. 4(11), as adopted).

What B3's message needs, then. B3's worked example asks one casual question and offers "saying no is completely fine and i'll keep them out of shot". It covers freely given and the no. To be specific and informed it also needs, in the same casual register: where the footage goes (YouTube, Instagram, the email, Shōmen's account if any), that it stays up (A4 record rule 3: nothing published is withdrawn), and how to change their mind. A4 record rule 3 already holds the exception: material exposing a third party is blurred or cut with a dated note. A partner who withdraws consent falls under that exception (his rule; basis: art. 81.º, n.º 2 and GDPR art. 7(3)). The agreement is kept in writing: the reply to the message is enough, kept in the season folder. B3's worked example carries these lines in its facts since 2 November 2026.

08The sponsored athletes: image rights for minors, club policy, disclosure

Open question 5, from A7. A7 §4 says the athletes' "image rights, consent, credit lines and anything about them as people belong to A8", its episode rule 7 that "The athletes appear only as A8 settles: by name, by role or not at all", Deliverable A part 2 that "No instalment goes out before A8's terms exist in writing", part 3 that "Sponsorship disclosures by the athletes themselves, and Portuguese and EU advertising rules: settled in A8", and its task table lists "A8's terms for the athletes in writing ... Before Thu 18 Feb 2027". The brief says the three sponsored athletes are minors. This section sets the rule and what the written terms must cover. It says nothing about the athletes themselves.

Why the bar is higher for minors. Art. 79.º applies to them as to anyone. Who consents for a minor is a matter of parental responsibilities in the Código Civil; those articles were not opened, so who must sign is a lawyer's question. The courts treat publication of minors' images as a protective matter: the Tribunal da Relação de Évora upheld an order that both parents refrain from posting photos or information that could identify their daughter, held that "os filhos não são coisas ou objectos pertencentes aos pais", and endorsed the view that there is a "perigo sério e real adveniente da divulgação de fotografias e informações de menores nas redes sociais" (TRE, proc. 789/13.7TMSTB-B.E1, 25 Jun 2015). Portugal set the age of digital consent at 13 (Lei 58/2019, art. 16.º, as Bird & Bird summarise it, read 2 Nov 2026); in February 2026 Parliament approved a bill that would set 16 as the age for autonomous access to social networks, with express parental consent from 13 (Euronews, 13 Feb 2026); its final status was not checked; that rule governs a child's consent to online services and does not by itself authorise a third party to publish the child. YouTube's child-safety policy says videos featuring minors "may have some features disabled", comments among them, and that the minor should be supervised by an adult (YouTube Help, Child safety policy). The Código da Publicidade requires that advertising "especialmente dirigida a menores deve ter sempre em conta a sua vulnerabilidade psicológica" (art. 14.º, n.º 1, compilation cited in §6); the rest of art. 14.º, which may bear on minors appearing in advertising, was not opened.

Club policy. No SC Braga policy on filming or images of athletes was found on 2 November 2026. Open; the club states it.

The rule (his rule; basis: art. 79.º and 81.º, the Évora judgment, GDPR art. 4(11) and 7(3)):

  • Until the written terms exist and are signed, the athletes appear not at all: no name, no face, no voice, no result. The series runs on what Shōmen did (A7's instalment rules 3 to 5 already centre the brand).
  • Once signed, they appear only to the extent the terms say, and by role is the default the terms start from ("three SC Braga athletes", as A7 already writes). By name, face or result is added only where the terms say yes to each, separately.
  • A withdrawal is honoured within a week: the material is cut or blurred with A4 record rule 3's dated note, on every platform where it ran.

  1. Parties: Shōmen (and him, if the channel uses the material), the holders of parental responsibilities for each athlete, who sign, and the athlete's own agreement, recorded.

09Pre-orders: withdrawal, cancellation and delivery

A7 §2 rule 4 says "Pre-order terms (delivery dates, cancellation, the right of withdrawal) are a matter of Portuguese and EU consumer law; they are settled in A8", and A7's inventory rules sell new products "on pre-order or made to order". Decreto-Lei 24/2014 governs distance sales in Portugal (consolidated text with Lei 47/2014, as printed in 2016, Faculdade de Direito de Lisboa; Decreto-Lei 109-G/2021 later amended nine of its articles, among them arts. 4.º, 10.º and 17.º, kept 14 days for distance contracts in art. 10.º, n.º 1, and left arts. 12.º and 19.º unchanged; whether it moved any alínea cited below, and any other later amendment, was not checked):

  • Before the order, the trader states "a data-limite em que o profissional se compromete a entregar o bem" and "a existência do direito de livre resolução do contrato, o respetivo prazo" (art. 4.º, n.º 1, als. i) and j)).
  • The consumer has 14 days to withdraw, running for goods from the day the consumer takes physical possession (art. 10.º, n.º 1, al. b)); if the withdrawal information was not given, the period is "de 12 meses a contar da data do termo do prazo inicial" (art. 10.º, n.º 2).
  • The trader refunds "No prazo de 14 dias a contar da data em que for informado da decisão de resolução" (art. 12.º, n.º 1).
  • There is no right of withdrawal for "bens confecionados de acordo com especificações do consumidor ou manifestamente personalizados" (art. 17.º, n.º 1, al. c)).
  • The trader fulfils the order "no prazo máximo de 30 dias" from the day after the contract, unless the parties agree otherwise (art. 19.º, n.º 1).

The Directive behind it says the same: "a period of 14 days to withdraw from a distance or off-premises contract, without giving any reason", with the exception for "goods made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised" (Directive 2011/83/EU, art. 9(1); art. 16(c)).

For Shōmen's pre-orders (his rules; basis: arts. 4.º, 10.º, 12.º, 17.º and 19.º): every pre-order page states a latest delivery date; where that date is more than 30 days away, the page says so and the buyer agrees to it at checkout; the page states the 14-day withdrawal right and the 14-day refund; and a standard-size garment made after the order is treated as carrying the right of withdrawal. Whether a standard garment produced to order counts as "made to the consumer's specifications" is a lawyer's question; offering withdrawal is the side that cannot breach art. 17.º.

10Music and competition footage on YouTube and Instagram

Music. YouTube's Content ID lets an owner of "a substantial body of original material" set what happens to a matching video: it "Blocks a video from being viewed", "Monetizes the video by running ads against it and sometimes sharing revenue with the uploader", or "Tracks the video's viewership statistics" (YouTube Help, How Content ID works). A claim typically does not bring a strike; a valid legal removal request does, and three strikes within 90 days make the account "subject to termination" (YouTube Help, Copyright strike basics). Tracks from YouTube's Audio Library can be used in monetised videos and "won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system"; Creative Commons tracks require crediting the artist in the description (YouTube Help, Audio Library). Fair use is a US concept; YouTube says other countries use different standards, the EU specific permitted categories, and "Giving credit to the owner of a copyrighted work won't by itself turn a non-transformative copy" fair (YouTube Help, Fair use).

On Instagram, Meta's 2020 guidance permits "traditional live music performances" and warns that "The greater the number of full-length recorded tracks in a video, the more likely it may be limited" (Instagram, 20 May 2020). A law firm's March 2026 note says the platforms' music libraries are "licensed only for private use" and "not ... intended for commercial influencers, corporate accounts or paid collaborations", with Meta's Sound Collection as the royalty-free option for business accounts (Osborne Clarke, 27 Mar 2026; secondary, and written on German copyright law; Meta's own music terms were not opened).

In a cohort of 556 Dutch half-marathon runners, 23.2% trained under 20 km a week, 42.0% 20–32 km and 34.8% over 32 km; the over-32 group finished 4.19 minutes faster than the 20–32 group after adjustment, with unadjusted means of 2:05:32, 2:02:03 and 1:55:26, and training volume was not significantly associated with new running injuries (Fokkema et al., Scand J Med Sci Sports, 2020; observational, peer-reviewed).
