PERSONAL BRAND · BRIEFS

Building a Marketable Personal Brand: the briefs

One brief per unit, self-contained: the shared preamble (reader, brand thesis, course goal, sourcing standard, tone, citation rules) is baked into every open brief. Copy, paste into a new Opus chat, one unit per chat. Written units show their note instead. The course itself is read in Course A.

A1

Positioning: the journey as a format

not written

The one sentence a stranger can repeat; the journey template (consumed → syllabus → process → record → artefact); tested against vlog ep. 1–2 and five creators who built a brand on a method rather than a topic.

Shipspositioning statement + channel rename + description

Opus brief
Write one unit of a self-study course in the same long-form report format as his history course: a long-form report for one reader. Length is whatever the topic needs (2,000–6,000 words); do not pad and do not cut to a target. Reader: Pedro, 35, Porto. Senior Director of Product Management (UX/AI) at a PE-backed software company; ADHD; ended competitive karate after the WIKF Global Cup (Oct 2026), still trains Wado-Ryū and kumite and coaches; next journeys are a sub-1:45 half marathon in spring/summer 2027, −15 kg, and a self-taught history course; owns Shōmen, a small karate-apparel brand; publishes in English with some Portuguese; has 5–7 h/week. Brand thesis (working name to be decided) — he gets consumed by a journey, builds a syllabus, records the process so he does not forget it, and ships the artefact; plurality is the register, each piece leads with one journey; ADHD is public under his name; failures stay in the record. Tone: direct, no flattery, no filler, no motivational language, no 'not X but Y' constructions. Course goal: by the end of the course the reader can state, in one sentence, who his audience is and what they pay for; run a publishing system that holds a bi-weekly cadence inside 5–7 h/week for twelve months; and read his own channel metrics against sourced benchmarks to decide, with evidence, whether to continue, change format or stop. Sourcing standard: every claim that carries a number, a mechanism or a recommendation is sourced; acceptable sources are peer-reviewed work, platform-official documentation or statements, large-sample vendor datasets (named, with sample size and date), and documented creator cases with verifiable numbers; creator anecdotes are labelled as such; correlational and vendor data is labelled as such; no uncited figures; no invented handles, names or numbers; if a claim cannot be sourced, say so and leave it out of the deliverable. A unit that reads as advice without evidence is a failed unit. Where the unit depends on current facts (prices, platform rules, model lineups), search before writing and date the facts. End with the unit's deliverable, built out in full, not described.

Unit A1, Positioning. Cover: what a positioning is and is not (a sentence a stranger can repeat vs a description of the person); why method-based brands survive variety and topic-based ones do not; the journey template as the recognisable format; three candidate umbrella names for the brand with the case for and against each; five verified creators whose brand is a method (name, platform, numbers, what their one sentence is); how the two existing vlog episodes ('Sometimes You Win, Sometimes You Learn. Mostly It Sucks'; 'Being a Better Athlete Made Me a Worse Person') already express or contradict the positioning. Deliverable: the positioning statement (one sentence plus a 60-word expansion), three channel-name options with one recommended, and a YouTube channel description and Instagram bio written in full.

Cover: the capture layer during a journey (dictated takes after sessions, journal entries, biometric screenshots, phone footage) and the rule that nothing is filmed twice; the recap layer (one long-form episode per milestone, 10–20 min); the derivative layer (2–3 Shorts per episode, 2–3 Instagram posts/week from the same material, one short weekly email); what a 'season' is and why one journey per season; how the Athens finale hands over to the half-marathon season; batching for an ADHD brain (one filming window, one editing window per fortnight).
